Binance hack, October 2026: a sourced incident timeline

Research published 1 November 2026 · am3rix · Read-only public evidence

This chronology separates transactions anyone can inspect on Ethereum from incident disclosures and third-party attribution. It is an independent review of published evidence, not an internal incident report.

Why the timeline matters

Public blockchains timestamp transfers and record transaction execution, but they do not record the first moment an attacker accessed a workstation, how signing approvals were obtained, or which human operated a wallet. A credible reconstruction must keep those categories apart.

Reported preparation: 28 October 2026

TRM Labs identified funds being staged and contracts tested before the unauthorized withdrawals. Those activities may show preparation by accounts later associated with the incident. Without independent identity evidence, the activity alone cannot prove who controlled them.

Outflows begin: 29 October

In its published analysis, TRM Labs places the beginning of unauthorized movement near 20:05 UTC, with activity across several networks. Transfer-time USD valuations differ across reports because asset prices, cut-off times and chain coverage differ. The widely reported approximately $6.3 billion figure is not computed by the Ethereum-only verifier in this repository.

Ethereum authorization and sweep

A type-4 authorization transaction appears at block 26,300,753. The later sweep transaction appears at block 26,300,770. This relative order can be checked with public block data. For details on what EIP-7702 changes, see the authorization mechanism guide.

How was access gained?

Binance initially described a possible compromised DevOps laptop and access to a withdrawal-related service container, later discussing additional compromise points. This was a preliminary company account. The public ledger neither confirms that initial access scenario nor reveals how the signatures were acquired.

Attribution and containment

The FBI's 31 October 2026 advisory attributes the attack to the DPRK-associated TraderTraitor cluster. Attribution depends on information beyond transaction metadata. Public reports also distinguish freezing certain stablecoin balances from recovering funds for the rightful owner; those are separate outcomes.

What this timeline does not establish

The identity of the individual operator, the full scope of compromised infrastructure and the precise trust-boundary failure cannot be proven from blockchain explorer data alone. Any update to these findings should be linked to a dated primary source.
